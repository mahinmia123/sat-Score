Digital SAT Frequently Asked Questions

The Digital SAT is scored on a scale between 400 and 1600. Your total score is the sum of your two section scores: Reading and Writing (200-800 points) and Math (200-800 points). Every question counts toward your points, but answering correctly in Module 1 is critical to triggering the higher-scoring Hard adaptive modules.

A good SAT score depends on your target universities. Generally, 1200+ is competitive for most state universities, 1400+ is excellent for top-tier national colleges (like UCLA, NYU, or Michigan), and 1500+ is exceptional, putting you in range for Ivies, Stanford, and MIT.

The Digital SAT operates on a two-module system. Everyone takes Module 1 first. Scoring well on Module 1 unlocks the 'Hard' track of Module 2, which allows you to reach a maximum score of 800. Scoring lower routes you to the 'Easy' track, which caps your maximum score at around 650.

The highest possible score on the SAT is 1600. This requires obtaining a perfect 800 on both the Reading & Writing section and the Math section.

To calculate your superscore, take your highest Math section score and your highest Reading & Writing section score across all your test dates and add them together. Most colleges accept and prefer your superscore.

Digital SAT scores are typically released online about 13-14 days after your test date. For summer or school-day test dates, the delay may vary slightly.

How scoring works

Your raw score (questions answered correctly) in Module 1 determines whether Module 2 is set to Easy or Hard. A Hard Module 2 opens up the 800 scoring potential, whereas scoring poorly leads to the Easy Module 2, capping maximum score potential.
